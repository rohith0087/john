# IDN Watch: a 60-second introduction

“Start with the health systems your sales team follows. IDN Watch retrieves updates from selected official public sources and turns them into consistent account developments, keeping the source, dates, and reported facts.

“An editor checks the update, corrects it where needed, and approves the version that sales will see. Shared rules then help prioritize follow-up based on the scale of the change, supplier relevance, a clear next step, recency, and evidence.

“The result fits into a small dashboard widget. A salesperson can see what changed, why it may matter, and what to check next. Opening the account brief adds the evidence, context, and a chart when comparable source figures are available.”

## An easy example

A health system announces a proposed hospital tower. That becomes a suggested follow-up to confirm approval, project scope, timing, and purchasing contacts. It is not treated as a confirmed order or an open procurement process.

## Set expectations before the demo

- This is a working proof of concept using bounded public-source collection, refreshed on demand.
- Target accounts are parent IDNs. Hospital developments are grouped under those IDNs.
- An editorial approval includes that version in the local sales feed; it does not publish to the client's platform or guarantee every fact.
- Ranking uses one shared rule set today; supplier-specific personalization is later work.
- A chart shows reported figures or a labeled, bounded scenario. It does not predict demand or revenue.

## Suggested demo path

1. Show the selected IDNs in Health systems.
2. Open a recommendation and explain its suggested next step.
3. Show its priority reasons and original source.
4. Expand into the account brief and, where supported, the capacity chart.
5. Finish with a compact widget layout beside other dashboard metrics.
